# 02 · Campaigns, Angles & Viral Playbook

## A. Core campaign angles (ranked)

### 1. "Eyes-Free Money" — the accessibility hero campaign
**Problem:** ~224,000 blind and ~750,000 visually impaired Kenyans largely depend on other people to transact — dictating PINs to shopkeepers, trusting relatives with balances. M-Pesa's IVR and braille statements help, but screen readers still choke on its app.
**Angle:** Ongea Pesa removes the middleman entirely. The user speaks; money moves; the phone confirms out loud; the face/voice biometric means nobody else can do it for them — or *to* them.
**Hero line:** *"For 970,000 Kenyans, sending money means trusting a stranger. Not anymore."*
**Assets:** documentary short (script V1 in doc 03), before/after user testimonials, partnership launch with Kenya Society for the Blind / Kenya Union of the Blind / inABLE.

### 2. "Sema Tu" — the speed/convenience mass-market campaign
Voice vs. thumbs. Everyone who's fat-fingered a paybill number is the audience. Fun, fast, Sheng-heavy. TikTok-native.
**Hero line:** *"Ukiweza kuongea, unaweza ku-bank."*

### 3. "Your Voice Is Your Password" — the security/tech campaign
Aimed at skeptics and press. Demo that a recording, a deepfake, or your twin brother can't move your money. Turn the biggest objection (is voice safe?) into the headline.

### 4. "Chama Bila Stress" — the group-money campaign
Voice-run chamas: start collections, check contributions, distribute payouts by speaking. Targets the treasurer of every chama in Kenya — the most stressed person in Kenyan finance.

### 5. "Mama Mboga & Mzee" campaign — low-literacy and elderly users
The quiet giant: users who *can* see but can't comfortably read menus. Voice removes the shame of asking for help.

## B. Viral mechanics & publicity stunts

1. **The Blindfold Challenge** — creators (and the public) try to send money with M-Pesa's app blindfolded, then with Ongea Pesa. Timer on screen. Duet/stitch format. This is the flagship viral loop: cheap, replicable, makes the point in 30 seconds.
2. **"PIN Yako Ni Siri?"** street interviews — ask people in town how many others know their M-Pesa PIN. The answers will be horrifying and shareable. Pivot: "Your voice can't be borrowed."
3. **Voice-only day** — James runs an entire business day (matatu, lunch, shopping) paying only by voice, vlogged. Founder-as-first-user content.
4. **Deepfake bounty** — public challenge: KES 100,000 to anyone who can break into a demo account with a cloned voice (controlled demo account, legal T&Cs reviewed first). Press gold if framed responsibly.
5. **The Handover** — film giving founder shirt #2 to the first visually impaired brand ambassador; launch their account live on stage at an accessibility event.
6. **White Cane Day activation (Oct 15)** and **Int'l Day of Persons with Disabilities (Dec 3)** — own these dates annually: free onboarding clinics with KSB/KUB, press invited.
7. **Radio-first launch** — our medium *is* audio. Vernacular radio (Radio Citizen, Ramogi, Inooro, Ghetto Radio) reaches exactly the users screens exclude. Run live on-air demos: DJ sends money by voice mid-show.
8. **Matatu SACCO pilot** — voice payments in one high-profile route; wrap 3 matatus in Ongea Pesa branding. Moving billboards + real transaction volume.

## C. PR hooks (pitch these as stories, not ads)

- "The Nairobi startup letting blind Kenyans bank without borrowing eyes" → Nation, Standard, TechCabal, Techpoint Africa, Disrupt Africa, BBC Africa, CNN Africa tech segments.
- "Kenya gave the world M-Pesa. This founder thinks the sequel is voice." → founder-profile pitch.
- "Can a deepfake steal your savings? We paid hackers to try." → after the bounty stunt.
- Data story: commission/compile a short survey on how many visually impaired Kenyans share PINs with helpers; release as a report. Owning a statistic = permanent citations.

## E. Measurement

North star: **weekly voice transactions**. Marketing metrics: cost per install, install→first-transaction rate, K-factor on challenge content, share of installs from accessibility-org referrals (track with referral codes per org/influencer).
